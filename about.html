Who I am

I am Justin Helmer. I write, record, and mix at home. Listeners know the records as Excavationpro; longer creative and mythic work uses Lightfather. I am Canadian, independent, and not interested in waiting for a label to decide whether a song is allowed to exist.

The catalog is experimental hip-hop and electronic texture: songs, instrumentals, sketches, and full projects. It is large because the practice is daily. Not every track is a single; the catalog is a body of work, not a playlist optimized for a chart.

Why the music is free

The first encounter should be a listen, not a negotiation. The public player is free, the stores are there if you want them, and the masters stay offline. Free listening is not a license to re-upload or sell the files; it is a permission to hear the work and share the door.

That model is explained in why free streams exist and in the Excavationpro essay. The short version: a preview behind a storefront is a sales pitch; a full stream is a relationship.

Live rooms

Kick, Rumble, and Twitch are treated as radio: a place to work, listen, and talk. They are not a hard sell. The live links are on the home page and inside the player’s live tab. If a room is offline, the player and the catalog are still there.

The other half: LYGO

LYGO is the agent and tool stack I publish beside the music: the console, SkillHub, the games, the lattice docs. It is not a replacement for the catalog; it is the other half of the workshop. The tools hub is the map.

How the catalog is made

Most records start in the same room where they finish. A drum pattern or a phrase becomes a rough arrangement, then a vocal, then a mix. The tools are ordinary: a DAW, a microphone, a small interface, and time. The constraint is deliberate. A home studio forces decisions; a large room can hide them.

Collaborators appear where they are credited. The catalog is not a band pretending to be one person, and it is not a solo myth that erases the people who played on it. If a record has a feature, the feature is named. If it is a solo piece, it is a solo piece.

The pace is daily rather than quarterly. Some tracks are sketches that exist to test an idea; some are finished songs; some are texture pieces that only make sense in a sequence. The catalog is allowed to contain all three because the practice is the point, not a release calendar.

Why this domain exists

There are three jobs and three places for them. The name portal is here: bio, catalog, press, lyrics, and the doors. The artist house is excavationpro.ca: essays, the rights model, and the workshop copy. The listening room is asiancoastline.com: the free player and the public catalog.

Keeping them separate is not bureaucracy. It means a listener can find the player without reading a bio, a journalist can find the bio without loading a 5 MB catalog, and the artist can change one surface without breaking the other two.

FAQ

Where are you based?

Canada. The catalog is recorded at home; the live rooms are online.

Do you play live?

The live rooms are the main live surface right now. They are radio-style sessions, not a tour schedule.

Is Excavationpro a band?

It is the recording project of Justin Helmer. The catalog is the work of one person with collaborators where credited.

Can I use a track in a project?

Ask first. Free listening is not a sync or commercial license. The contact page is the route.
