One catalog, three names

Justin Helmer is the name on the door. The records go out as Excavationpro. Longer creative and mythic work sometimes uses Lightfather. The three names are not separate catalogs; they are layers of the same practice. If you came from a store badge, a lyric, or a live room, you are in the right place.

The music is experimental hip-hop and electronic texture, recorded and mixed at home without a label deciding what a song is allowed to be. The catalog is large because the practice is daily, not because every track is a single. It includes songs, instrumentals, sketches, and full projects.

How a record gets made

A track usually starts with a drum pattern, a sample-free texture, or a phrase that will not leave. The writing and recording happen in the same room; the mix follows the arrangement rather than sanding it into a format. Some pieces are built to be songs, some are built to be listened to as texture. The distinction is intentional.

Masters stay in the vault. The public stream is a compressed listening copy. That is the line: open listening, closed ownership. The streams vs masters guide explains the model in full.

Where to listen

The canonical free player is asiancoastline.com/listen.html. It carries the public catalog, search, queue, radio, favorites, and an installed-app shell. No account is required. This domain links to that player instead of running a second copy of the same 11,000-track catalog; one listening room is easier to maintain and easier to trust.

If you want the catalog in the stores you already use, the two official smart links are ffm.to/justin-helmer for the name and ffm.to/eovnvo9 for Excavationpro. Spotify, YouTube Music, and Audius are linked from the home page. The free player and the stores are different doors to the same work.

Lyrics and live rooms

The lyric vault holds the words with a search and a mini player for mapped streams. The live rooms on Kick, Rumble, and Twitch are treated as radio: a place to work, listen, and talk, not a hard sell. Links are on the home page and in the player’s live tab.

What to start with

  • If you want songs: open the player, search an album title, and let the queue run.
  • If you want words: start in the lyric vault and follow the mapped stream on a track.
  • If you want context: read why the music is free.
  • If you want the whole map: use the tools hub.

A catalog, not a release cycle

The catalog grows by accumulation rather than by campaign. A project may sit beside a one-off sketch; an instrumental may be as finished as a song. That is why the player shows a list rather than a tidy discography. The list is the honest shape of the work.

If you are new, do not try to hear everything. Pick a title that sounds like a mood, play three tracks, and notice whether the production is the point or the writing is. The catalog has both. The search box and the album filter are there so you can follow one thread instead of drinking from the firehose.

What the catalog is not

It is not a licensing library. A free stream is a listening copy; it is not a sync license, a sample clearance, or a commercial use. The stores are the purchase route where a release exists. The essays at excavationpro.ca explain the rights model in plain language.

It is also not a promise that every track will stay online forever. The public catalog is maintained, not frozen. The player’s ledger records a snapshot; a snapshot is a reference, not a guarantee of playback.

FAQ

Is the music really free?

Yes. The public player is free to hear; no account is required. Optional support helps hosting.

Can I download the tracks?

The player is a listening room, not a download store. Store links are the legal purchase route where available.

Why three names?

Justin Helmer is the person, Excavationpro is the recording project, Lightfather is the longer-form creative layer. Same catalog family.

Where are the masters?

In the artist’s vault. The public stream is a compressed copy; the master is the archival source.
